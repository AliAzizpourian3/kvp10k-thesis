\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

\section{Cross-Result Interpretation}
\label{sec:interpretation}

Chapter~\ref{ch:exresults} gives an immediate interpretation after each main
result. This section relates those results across evidence types and examines
their mechanisms, limitations, and implications.

\subsection{Entity Classification and Pair Extraction}

For the final LayoutLMv3-based span-level discriminative model (V4),
Tables~\ref{tab:v4-entity} and~\ref{tab:v4-results} report validation
token-level entity micro F1 of $0.813$ and validation Regular text+location
pair F1 of $0.331$. The measures use different units: entity F1 evaluates
labelled tokens, while pair F1 also requires correct span construction and
relation decoding. The numerical difference is therefore not a like-for-like
estimate of linker performance. Together with the verified checkpoint-15
examples, it nevertheless supports the interpretation that the transition
from entity classification to complete pair extraction is a major internal
limitation. The evidence does not isolate linker errors from upstream label and
span errors.

An incorrect entity label or boundary can change the candidate set before the
linker acts, and a correct pair of entity spans can still receive an incorrect
relation prediction. Because V4 copies predicted text from the extracted input
tokens, it also cannot generate text that is absent from the input. This
constraint does not prevent label, boundary, or relation errors.

\subsection{Candidate Granularity and Development Evidence}

V1 forms relation candidates between individual \texttt{KEY} and
\texttt{VALUE} tokens. Its $N_KN_V$ construction creates a dense candidate
space and uses a finer unit than the annotated span-level relations, as
explained in Section~\ref{sec:v1-imbalance}. Grouping contiguous entity tokens
into spans reduces the candidate space and makes the candidate unit closer to
the annotation unit. However, the preserved V1 and V2 paths also differ in
span source, bounding-box filtering, and relation-label construction. Their
development evidence therefore does not isolate candidate granularity as a
causal factor.

\subsection{System-Level Comparison and Output Coverage}

The same-page comparison in Table~\ref{tab:main-system-comparison} gives
Regular text+location F1 of $0.657$ for the reconstructed Mistral-7B reference
system and $0.329$ for V4. Under the fixed shared evaluation protocol, Mistral
is also higher in both precision and recall. V4's lower recall indicates a
higher missed-relation rate, while its lower precision indicates that a larger
proportion of its accepted relations is incorrect. Its gap therefore appears
on both sides of the precision--recall trade-off.

This comparison shows that the reconstructed Mistral-7B reference system is
stronger than V4 under the shared protocol. It does not establish that
generative systems are generally superior to discriminative systems. The two
systems differ in model capacity, training coverage, training objective, and
output representation.
The local Mistral result is also not directly comparable with the published
KVP10k Mistral-7B baseline because the page coverage, extracted input text,
prompt, and fine-tuning implementation differ.

V4's direct decoder supports Regular pairs only. This output restriction
contributes to its All text+location F1 of $0.214$ because it emits no Unkeyed
or Unvalued entries. No synchronised checkpoint-15 recovery artifact exists,
so the final evidence cannot quantify whether post-processing would reduce
this coverage limitation.

\subsection{Annotation-Geometry Strata}

The final annotation-geometry comparison is reported in
Table~\ref{tab:exp1-cluster}. V4 has higher text and text+location F1 in
Cluster~1, while its location F1 differs only slightly between the two groups.
The reconstructed Mistral-7B reference system has higher F1 in Cluster~0 for
all three matching modes. This opposite ordering is compatible with different
sensitivity to annotation geometry, but the analysis does not identify a
causal mechanism. The groups are not semantic document types, and the training
silhouette coefficient of $0.222$ indicates weak separation
(Section~\ref{sec:layout_clustering}). The cluster pattern must therefore
remain exploratory.

\section{Interpretation of Development Diagnostics}
\label{sec:root-cause-analysis}

The diagnostics in Section~\ref{sec:res567} identify three issues that affected
candidate construction and relation supervision. The first V2 path formed
training spans from predicted entity labels while ground-truth annotations
supplied the relation targets. This produced a mismatch between the candidate
spans and their supervision. The later teacher-forced V2 configuration and V4
use ground-truth spans during training and predicted spans during validation
and inference. This aligns training candidates with the targets, but leaves a
train--inference difference in span source.

The historical V2 path also rescaled prepared $0$--$100$ coordinates by page
dimensions before mapping them to the LayoutLMv3 model grid. The additional
quantisation could create zero-width or zero-height model-input boxes, which a
strict validity predicate then removed. In addition, the historical relation
label generator created all pairings between the words of a key and the words
of its value instead of one representative relation per annotation. These
mechanisms could remove valid candidates or make relation supervision denser
than the annotated relations.

V4 changes coordinate handling, bounding-box filtering, and relation-label
construction together. Its final result therefore cannot quantify the effect
of any single correction. The span-level formulation remains useful because
it reduces the candidate space and makes the sparse relation target easier to
inspect, but controlled ablations are necessary to separate this effect from
the linker design and the other pipeline changes.

\section{Qualitative Error Analysis}
\label{sec:error_analysis}

The cases in Table~\ref{tab:v4-qualitative-errors} illustrate two routes to an
incorrect final pair. A missing or merged entity span can prevent the intended
relation from entering the candidate set, while competing plausible spans can
lead to an incorrect relation decision. This distinction helps explain why
the difference between entity and pair metrics cannot be assigned to the
linker alone. The selected cases are illustrative rather than a statistical
sample, and annotation ambiguity limits conclusions from individual examples.

\section{Relation-Modeling Context and Experimental Scope}
\label{sec:relation_context}

The dense V1 candidate space and the verified V4 case involving a competing
neighbouring value motivate relation designs that impose more structure than
independent local pair decisions. SPADE~\cite{spade} models directed dependencies over document
tokens, while BROS~\cite{bros} combines relative spatial representations with
a SPADE-based decoder. GeoLayoutLM~\cite{geolayoutlm},
KVPFormer~\cite{kvpformer}, and LayoutPointer~\cite{layoutpointer} provide
further geometric, coarse-to-fine, or pointer-based alternatives.

These methods were not implemented in this thesis, and their reported results
were not produced under the prepared 581-page KVP10k protocol. Their numerical
results are therefore not treated as directly comparable baselines. They
instead define evidence-based directions for testing whether more structured
relation modelling improves the current classifier-linker pipeline. The scope
of the experimental comparison is defined in
Section~\ref{sec:experimental_scope}.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{Current Limitations}

Several limitations constrain the interpretation of the results. First, the
KVP10k paper reports 10{,}707 pages~\cite{kvp10k}. In the released annotations
used here, Section~\ref{sec:kvp10k} counts 9{,}656 unique training pages and
1{,}051 unique test pages. The local preparation pipeline retained 5{,}389
training pages and 581 test pages. The fixed development split uses 4{,}851
pages for model training and 538 for validation. The reconstructed Mistral-7B reference
system separately excludes 404 prepared pages with empty targets and therefore
uses 4{,}985 training pages. The exclusions depend partly on PDF availability
and usable native text, so the reported results might not generalise to the
excluded pages or to scanned documents without a text layer. The pipeline uses
PyMuPDF native PDF text extraction~\cite{pymupdf} rather than optical character
recognition.

Second, the reconstructed Mistral-7B reference system is not an exact
reproduction of the published system. The released implementation and
configuration are documented in the KVP10k
repository~\cite{kvp10k_repository};
Section~\ref{sec:mistral-implementation} describes the corresponding
differences in the reconstructed system used here. Third, V4 uses constant
blank visual input. It evaluates textual and layout features but does not use
document-specific image content. Fourth, inputs are limited to 512 tokens and
one page. Truncation can remove later content, and the current architecture
does not model cross-page relations. Finally, the qualitative cases are
illustrative, and annotation ambiguity can affect their interpretation.

\subsection{Future Directions}

Controlled ablations should test span source, bounding-box filtering,
relation-label construction, and linker design separately while retaining the
final corrected coordinate mapping and fixed data splits. This would estimate
the contribution of each design choice to complete-pair performance.
Additional factorial experiments would be required to determine whether these
effects interact. A second comparison should test whether one-to-one or
constrained decoding reduces incompatible local relation decisions. Training
can also include deliberately selected plausible but incorrect neighbouring
values as negative examples. This targets the type of local ambiguity
illustrated by the saved V4 case, but its effect requires controlled
evaluation. Future variants should test explicit row and column relations
against the current pairwise displacement features and the structured methods
identified in Section~\ref{sec:relation_context}.

The output design also requires controlled evaluation. Learned Unkeyed and
Unvalued classes could give the final decoder direct special-category coverage.
A calibration study should measure whether predicted relation probabilities
reflect observed correctness before a threshold is selected on validation
data. Longer-input and multi-page variants are needed to evaluate truncation
and cross-page relations, while an experiment with rendered document images is
needed to measure the contribution of genuine visual features. All these items
are future work; they were not implemented in V4.
