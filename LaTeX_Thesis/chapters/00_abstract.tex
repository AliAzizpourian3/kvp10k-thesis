  {\selectlanguage{english}Abstract}

\normalfont

\vspace{0.5cm}

This thesis presents a comprehensive study on automatic \kvp{} extraction from business documents using a layout-aware discriminative approach. We develop and evaluate a LayoutLMv3-based encoder using text and layout inputs, augmented with a learned linking mechanism for accurate key-value association.

\textbf{Background:} Business document processing remains a significant challenge in document understanding, requiring accurate extraction of structured information from semi-structured layouts. Traditional approaches rely on template matching or rule-based systems, limiting generalization to diverse document types. Recent generative baselines using large language models achieve reasonable text extraction but lack spatial grounding and can generate unsupported text.

\textbf{Method:} We propose a discriminative approach combining a LayoutLMv3-based encoder using text and layout inputs with a two-head architecture: an entity classifier that assigns each token a role (key, value, or other) and a learned linker that pairs predicted key spans with their corresponding value spans. We investigate two linking granularities: token-level linking (V1) and span-level linking (V2), motivated by the observation that entity linking operates naturally at the span level. Our approach is evaluated on the KVP10k dataset comprising 10,707 unique pages spanning invoices, forms, and receipts.

\textbf{Evaluation:} We follow IBM's released KVP10k benchmark: type-aware one-to-one pair matching with NED $<0.2$, IoU $\geq0.3$ in the executable code, and macro-averaged per-page precision and recall. Entity extraction and key-value linking are evaluated separately.

\textbf{Results:} The discriminative LayoutLMv3 model achieves entity F1 of 0.827 and prevents generation of text absent from the OCR input. Token-level linking (V1) yields near-zero pooled diagnostic F1 (0.008) under extreme class imbalance ($\sim$450:1). Span-level linking reduces the imbalance but initially fails because of three compounding data-pipeline bugs. After correction, V4 reaches regular-KVP macro F1 $=0.334$ for text and $0.309$ for text+location, compared with the paper's $0.659$ and $0.611$. In its primary configuration V4 emits regular linked pairs only; a post-hoc decode-time recovery optionally extends coverage to the unkeyed and unvalued categories at markedly lower accuracy. Diagnostics identify relation modeling within the current biaffine-linker pipeline as the main remaining bottleneck.

\textbf{Keywords:} Document Understanding, Key-Value Pair Extraction, LayoutLMv3, Span-Level Linking, Layout-Aware Learning, Information Extraction
