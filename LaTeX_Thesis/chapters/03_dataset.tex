\section{KVP10k Dataset}
\label{sec:kvp10k}

The KVP10k dataset \cite{kvp10k} is a large-scale benchmark for \kvp{} extraction from business documents. It contains 10,707 unique pages: 9,656 training pages and 1,051 test pages. The released data contain 48,280 training and 5,255 test annotation rows because pages have multiple annotator copies. The pages span diverse document types including invoices, forms, and receipts.

\subsection{Data Collection and Annotation Format}
The dataset was collected by IBM Research from real-world business documents, with manual annotation of bounding boxes and key-value relationships. Each raw row contains an annotator copy for a page together with a list of annotations. Fields relevant to this analysis include a \texttt{label}, optional polygon \texttt{coordinates} (a list of $\{x, y\}$ points), and an optional \texttt{attributes.Linking} reference that encodes a key--value relationship. Coordinate availability differs across annotator copies of the same page.

The downstream preparation pipeline operates on unique page hashes and produced 5{,}389 usable pages from the KVP10k training split and 581 usable test pages. These prepared pages are distinct from the duplicated raw rows used by the Stage~2 analysis below.

\section{Page-Level Annotation-Geometry Clustering}
\label{sec:layout_clustering}

We perform exploratory unsupervised clustering of page-level annotation-box geometry. Raw rows are first grouped by \texttt{hash\_name}. For each unique training page, we retain the annotator copy with the largest number of usable polygon coordinates. Pages for which no copy contains usable coordinates are excluded rather than represented by an all-zero vector. This yields 9{,}124 coordinate-bearing pages from the 9{,}656 unique training pages; 532 pages have unavailable annotation geometry. The fitted groups are geometric regimes, not document genres or independently validated semantic document types.

\subsection{Feature Extraction}
\label{subsec:features}

We extract 12 distinct geometric summary features per selected page (Table~\ref{tab:features}). The original extractor emitted 13 columns, but its \texttt{density} column was exactly equal to \texttt{total\_area}; removing that duplicate avoids assigning page coverage twice the weight during standardization and clustering.

\subsection{K-means Clustering}
\label{subsec:kmeans}

We standardize the 12 features with a training-fitted \texttt{StandardScaler} and apply K-means clustering \cite{kmeans} with seed 42 and 10 initializations. To select the number of clusters, we sweep $k \in \{2,\dots,10\}$ and evaluate the silhouette score (primary), Davies--Bouldin index, Calinski--Harabasz score, and inertia curve.

Figure~\ref{fig:stage2_optimal_k} summarizes the selection outcome. The prespecified primary criterion peaks at $k=2$, with silhouette $0.222$; the Calinski--Harabasz score is also largest at $k=2$. Davies--Bouldin continues to improve for larger $k$, so the evidence for a clean two-group partition is limited. We retain $k=2$ as the simplest primary-criterion solution and treat the resulting groups as exploratory strata rather than sharply separated document classes.

\subsection{Use in Later Stages}
\label{subsec:cluster_use}

The scaler and K-means model are fitted only on the 9{,}124 coordinate-bearing
training pages. Test rows are grouped by page hash and reduced with the same
largest-coordinate-count rule. The frozen scaler and K-means model assign 588
of the 1{,}051 unique test pages to Cluster~0 and 396 to Cluster~1. The 67 pages
with no usable polygon geometry are marked \emph{geometry unavailable} rather
than projected from an out-of-distribution zero vector. In the 581-page prepared
evaluation set, the corresponding counts are 319, 213, and 49. Per-cluster
results in later chapters are recomputed from saved predictions and ground truth;
the overall benchmark scores are unchanged (Section~\ref{sec:stage3-per-cluster}).
An implementation excerpt for applying the frozen clustering model is provided
in Appendix~\ref{app:cluster-assignment}.

\section{Key-Value Link Analysis}
\label{sec:kv_analysis}

We analyze spatial relationships between linked \kvps{} to inform model design.

\subsection{Spatial Proximity}

For each key-value pair with linking annotation, we compute the Euclidean distance between bounding box centers:

\begin{equation}
d_{kv} = \sqrt{(x_v - x_k)^2 + (y_v - y_k)^2}
\end{equation}

where $(x_k, y_k)$ and $(x_v, y_v)$ are the normalized center coordinates of the key and value boxes.

On a 200-row annotation subset containing 755 links,\footnote{The subset was formed from the first 200 training rows with non-empty annotation lists. It represents 70 unique page hashes, of which 69 contribute coordinate-bearing rows. This exploratory raw-row subset is distinct from the 581-page evaluation set used in Chapter~\ref{ch:exresults}.} linked pairs tend to be proximate: the mean key--value distance is $\mu_d = 0.120$, the median is $\tilde{d} = 0.095$, and the 90th percentile is $d_{90} = 0.239$ (all in normalized coordinate units). The proximity pattern suggests that spatial features may be informative for linking.

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:dataset_summary}

The clustering indicates two weakly separated annotation-geometry regimes: one with more boxes and broader spatial spread, and another with fewer boxes in a more compact region. These groups are used only as exploratory geometric strata and should not be interpreted as document types or text-density classes. Separately, the 200-row spatial audit shows that linked \kvps{} are often relatively close on the page. This proximity supports the use of spatial features in the linker, while the cluster assignments are retained for descriptive performance analysis.
